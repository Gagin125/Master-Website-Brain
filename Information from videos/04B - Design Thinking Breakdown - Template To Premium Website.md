# Design Thinking Breakdown - Template To Premium Website

Source video: https://youtu.be/CqSXDZajbyE?si=YNnAxcRg1CqNG9zP
Companion note: `04 - Transforming Templates With Typography Imagery Details and Motion - Extracted Principles.md`

## What This Note Is For

This note is not the usual key-points extraction.

This note captures how the designer was thinking while transforming a boring template into a premium concept. The goal is to preserve the design reasoning so future AI agents can imitate the thought process, not just repeat the checklist.

## The Core Mental Move

He did not ask:

"How do I make this template prettier?"

He asked, implicitly:

"What stronger world can this boring product belong to?"

That is the key difference.

The product was an AC remote. On its own, that is not visually exciting. A weaker designer would show the remote cleanly, add a nice font, and call it done. He instead reframed the remote through related emotional and symbolic worlds:

- Temperature
- Coolness
- Fashion
- Perfume
- Luxury object photography
- Desert heat
- Clouds
- Water droplets
- Wind
- Blue light
- Human emotion

The final design feels premium because the remote stops being only a remote. It becomes an object inside an authored visual universe.

## Step 1 Thinking: Typography Before Decoration

He begins with typography because type is the fastest way to change the perceived quality of a layout.

The template itself is extremely basic:

- Centered hero
- Background image
- Centered heading
- Button

Instead of immediately changing the layout, he tests whether the same structure can feel better through type alone.

His thought process:

1. If the font is weak, everything else has to work harder.
2. If the font is strong, even a simple layout can feel intentional.
3. The words themselves affect the visual feeling.
4. Hierarchy changes the brand personality.
5. Duplicate the layout many times and let comparison reveal the best direction.

He explores:

- Bold uppercase type
- Lighter display type
- Small supporting words
- Giant type filling the screen
- Minimal elegant versions
- Fashion/editorial feeling
- Netflix-title feeling

The important behavior is not the exact font choice. The important behavior is that he lets typography explore different personalities before committing.

## What He Is Really Testing With Typography

He is testing brand temperature.

Different type treatments answer different questions:

- Is this product bold or refined?
- Is it technical or emotional?
- Is it fashion-like or utility-like?
- Is it playful or serious?
- Is it mass-market or premium?
- Should the product feel quiet or loud?

He is not just choosing what "looks nice." He is choosing what kind of brand the product becomes.

## Implementation Lesson For Future Sites

When starting a site, especially from a template, do not accept the first typographic direction.

Create 5 to 10 type-only hero explorations before touching imagery:

- One quiet luxury version
- One bold editorial version
- One friendly local version
- One high-contrast premium version
- One minimal version
- One expressive/artistic version

Then choose based on brand truth, not personal preference alone.

## Step 2 Thinking: Imagery Is Concept, Not Decoration

After type, he moves to imagery.

This is where the thinking becomes most important.

He does not search "AC remote website inspiration." That would trap him inside obvious category references.

Instead, he asks:

"What other visual worlds can express the feeling of this product?"

That is why he looks at:

- Fashion photography
- Product photography
- Perfume ads
- Surreal compositions
- Abstract images
- Lighting references
- Human emotional expressions

He uses the product's function as a creative bridge:

AC remote -> temperature -> cool/warm -> clouds/desert/wind/water/blue light

AC remote shape -> perfume bottle -> luxury object -> fashion/editorial photography

This is strategic metaphor building.

## The Product Translation Method

When a product or business is visually boring, translate it into related worlds:

1. What does it do?
2. What feeling does it create?
3. What physical sensations are connected to it?
4. What other premium categories share its shape, mood, or ritual?
5. What visual symbols can express that feeling indirectly?

For the remote:

- It controls temperature.
- Temperature connects to wind, heat, cold, moisture, clouds.
- The object is small and handheld.
- Small handheld premium objects include perfume, cosmetics, jewelry, watches.
- So the visual world can borrow from perfume/fashion/product luxury.

That is the leap that makes the design feel authored.

## Implementation Lesson For Future Sites

Before choosing photos, create a "visual world map."

Example for a cafe:

- Coffee -> morning ritual -> warm wood, steam, hands, silence, books
- Old town -> stone, windows, street texture, slower pace
- Sweet drinks -> color accents, glass, light, freshness
- Local cafe -> real faces, imperfect tables, lived-in warmth

Example for a restaurant:

- Fine dining -> restraint, darkness, close crops, table rituals
- Baltic/Northern food -> stone, fire, wood, seasonal ingredients
- Private events -> people, ceremony, glass, warm lighting

The visual world should come before the asset selection.

## Step 3 Thinking: AI Images Need Curation And Rejection

He shows that the first AI images are not good.

This is important because it reveals a professional attitude toward AI:

AI is not the designer.
AI is a generator of raw material.

He changes:

- Prompts
- Reference image
- Models
- Style direction
- Composition
- Emotional intensity
- Lighting
- Distance from subject

He also labels some outputs as failures.

That rejection step is essential.

## What He Accepts In An Image

He accepts images that have at least one strong design asset:

- Emotion
- Lighting
- Composition
- Symbolic connection
- Product visibility
- Negative space for type
- Strong crop
- Interesting atmosphere

He does not need every generated image to be perfect. Sometimes he keeps one because the lighting works. Sometimes because the composition works. Sometimes because the emotion works.

The designer is harvesting useful parts from the generation process.

## Implementation Lesson For Future AI Image Work

Do not prompt once and use the result.

Use this loop:

1. Gather non-web references.
2. Define the visual world.
3. Generate many directions.
4. Reject weak outputs quickly.
5. Keep only images with a clear reason.
6. Test them with the actual typography.
7. Crop and adjust them for the layout.
8. Decide if real photography would be more trustworthy.

For real client sites, use AI images carefully. They can help with speculative concepts, but real businesses often need real assets for trust.

## Step 4 Thinking: Type And Image Must Negotiate

Once he has imagery, he places it with the typography.

This is not a simple "background image behind text" step.

He checks:

- Does the text hide the product?
- Is there enough contrast?
- Is the product visible?
- Is the focal point in the right place?
- Does the image have room for the headline?
- Does the composition feel balanced?
- Does the crop create premium space?

He moves the subject. He duplicates the product. He uses dark negative space. He chooses compositions where the text and image can coexist.

This is where many AI designs fail: they pick a beautiful image and a beautiful font, but they do not make them negotiate with each other.

## Implementation Lesson For Future Sites

Every hero image should be tested with the actual hero text.

A good image that does not support the headline is not a good hero image.

Check:

- Product/face/object visibility
- Text contrast
- Mobile crop
- Focal point
- Emotional tone
- Space for CTA
- Whether the image supports the message

## Step 5 Thinking: Small Details Make The Template Disappear

He does not radically redesign the template. He adds details that make it feel custom:

- Navigation
- Logo placement
- Long divider lines
- Split headline treatment
- Bottom information grid
- Rotated text
- Scroll cue
- Button shape changes
- Grid lines

These details work because they are compositional.

They are not random decoration.

They solve problems:

- Fill dead space
- Balance the image
- Add editorial rhythm
- Break boring symmetry
- Create a custom visual signature
- Make the page feel intentionally art-directed

## The Rule Behind Details

Details should answer one of these:

- Does it guide the eye?
- Does it balance the composition?
- Does it support the concept?
- Does it clarify the interface?
- Does it add a memorable brand touch?

If not, it is decoration and should probably be removed.

## Implementation Lesson For Future Sites

When a design feels generic, do not immediately redesign the whole page.

Try adding one or two precise authorship details:

- A custom line system
- A crop motif
- A strong navigation treatment
- A typographic split
- A section number system
- A unique hover behavior
- A recurring shape from the brand
- A subtle scroll cue

But keep the details disciplined.

## Step 6 Thinking: Motion Only After Static Quality

Motion is the final layer.

He adds animation after:

- Typography works
- Imagery works
- Composition works
- Details work

The motion feels premium because it reveals an already-strong design.

If the static design were weak, the animation would only make the weakness move.

## Implementation Lesson For Future Sites

Motion should be used for:

- Entrance rhythm
- Guided attention
- Premium tactility
- Revealing hierarchy
- Making the page feel alive

Motion should not be used to:

- Distract from weak layout
- Cover bad typography
- Hide poor imagery
- Impress designers at the expense of users

## The Real Formula

The video's real formula is:

Template structure + typographic identity + symbolic visual world + curated image iteration + composition correction + authored details + restrained motion = premium result.

Not:

Template + nice font + AI image + animation = premium result.

The missing ingredient is judgment between every step.

## How I Should Apply This As Codex

When building future websites with Dovydas, I should not simply ask for assets and assemble sections.

I should think like this:

1. What is the current design's generic base?
2. What decisions make it look generic?
3. What is the brand's emotional world?
4. What non-web visual worlds could express it?
5. What typography direction fits that world?
6. What images support the message and composition?
7. What small details would make it feel authored?
8. What motion is needed only after the static design is strong?
9. What should be rejected because it is impressive but not right?

## What This Changes In The Brain

The brain should not only store design rules.

It should store design moves:

- Reframe boring product as premium object.
- Borrow from adjacent visual worlds.
- Make type establish brand personality first.
- Use AI imagery as exploration, not final truth.
- Make type and image negotiate.
- Add details as authorship, not decoration.
- Animate only after static composition succeeds.

## Strongest Takeaway

The designer did not beat the template by making a more complex layout.

He beat the template by making stronger decisions than the template made for him.

That is the difference between template use and template dependency.
