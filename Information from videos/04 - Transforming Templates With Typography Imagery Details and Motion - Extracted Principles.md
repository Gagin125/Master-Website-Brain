## Core Idea

Templates are not the enemy. Unconsidered design decisions are the enemy.

A boring template can become premium if the designer layers strong decisions on top of it:

1. Typography
2. Imagery and art direction
3. Composition details
4. Motion

The underlying layout can stay simple. The premium feeling comes from what the designer does with type, words, visual concept, image crop, atmosphere, spacing, detail, and motion.

## Visual Inspection Notes

The video showed a plain centered hero template from a component library being transformed into multiple premium-looking concepts for an AC remote brand.

The visual progression was clear:

- Start with a generic centered hero.
- Try multiple type treatments until the layout already feels premium without images.
- Build an inspiration board from fashion, product, perfume, surreal imagery, and lighting references.
- Generate and iterate product imagery, rejecting weak early outputs.
- Combine type with image compositions.
- Add small design details like navigation, dividing lines, rotated copy, bottom grids, scroll cues, and shaped buttons.
- Add a smooth intro animation so the final result feels alive.

The strongest lesson is that the template became unrecognizable because the design decisions became specific.

## The Four-Step Transformation Process

### 1. Typography First

The first transformation lever is typography.

Typography includes three separate decisions:

- The font choice
- The actual words
- The hierarchy, size, weight, spacing, and placement

The creator shows that a plain template can already feel premium before any imagery is added if the typography is strong.

Reusable principle:

Before looking for decoration, make the typography carry the design.

### 2. Words Are A Visual Asset

The video does not treat copy as an afterthought. The words are tested visually:

- "The Remote"
- "Cooler by Design"
- Temperature-related phrasing
- Short product statements

Changing only the words and hierarchy changes the whole feeling of the design.

Reusable principle:

Copy is not just information. In a premium hero, copy is part of the composition.

### 3. Test Many Type Directions

The creator duplicates the design repeatedly in Figma and tests:

- Big uppercase type
- Minimal smaller type
- Bold type
- Lighter type
- Centered compositions
- Fashion-like layouts
- Netflix-title-like layouts

He does not try to solve the design in one version.

Reusable principle:

Premium direction often appears through visible iteration, not a single clever choice.

### 4. Strong Fonts Make Everything Easier

The video recommends using high-quality font foundries and not defaulting to weak or generic free fonts.

Important correction for the brain:

This does not mean all free fonts are bad. It means type must be chosen deliberately. Many free fonts can work, but default, overused, or poorly matched fonts make templates look generic.

Better rule:

Use fonts with a clear reason, strong personality, good readability, and enough range for hierarchy.

## Imagery And Art Direction

### 1. Look Outside Web Design

The creator does not start image inspiration by looking only at websites.

He looks at:

- Fashion photography
- Lifestyle product imagery
- Perfume advertising
- Abstract compositions
- Product lighting
- Surreal visual references
- Painting-like compositions

This matters because web-only inspiration often produces web-looking results. Non-web inspiration creates more memorable direction.

Reusable principle:

Use non-web visual worlds to give a website a less generic visual language.

### 2. Translate The Product Into Another World

The AC remote is visually boring. The creator makes it interesting by asking:

- What if this remote were treated like a fashion object?
- What if it were treated like perfume?
- What if temperature became clouds, desert, wind, water droplets, or blue light?
- What if the remote became emotional instead of technical?

This is the real creative move.

Reusable principle:

When the product is boring, do not present it literally. Translate it into an emotional or symbolic world connected to what it does.

### 3. Build An Inspiration Board Before Generating Or Designing

The creator collects references into Figma first.

He does not prompt blindly. He gathers:

- Lighting references
- Compositional references
- Mood references
- Product-handling references
- Texture references
- Color references

Reusable principle:

An AI image prompt should come after visual research, not before it.

### 4. AI Image Generation Requires Iteration

The creator openly shows that the first AI outputs were not good.

He improves results by:

- Uploading a reference image of the remote
- Trying different prompts
- Trying different image models
- Testing different compositions
- Rejecting failures
- Keeping only images with usable emotion, lighting, or composition

Reusable principle:

Never accept the first AI image output as final. Treat AI imagery like a creative exploration tool, not a finished asset machine.

### 5. Image Concepts Should Connect To Product Meaning

The strongest image concepts were not random. They connected to temperature:

- Clouds
- Desert heat
- Blue light
- Water droplets
- Windy silk scarf
- Cool/dark atmosphere

Reusable principle:

Surreal or artistic imagery should still be anchored to the product's actual meaning.

## Composition And Layout

### 1. Combine Type And Image, Then Fix The Tension

After selecting images, the creator places them behind the typography and checks whether the layout works.

Problems discovered:

- Text can hide the product.
- Product can sit in the wrong focal area.
- Image can need recropping.
- Centered text may need a dark area behind it.
- A single image may need duplication or manipulation to preserve product visibility.

Reusable principle:

Image selection is not enough. The crop and focal point must serve the typography and message.

### 2. Break The Template With Small Details

The creator adds small design moves:

- Navigation
- Logo
- Thin divider lines
- Split headline treatment
- Scroll cue
- Bottom information columns
- Rotated side text
- Grid lines
- Rounded/pill button details

These details make the design feel custom while keeping the base structure simple.

Reusable principle:

Customization does not always require a new layout. A few precise details can make a template feel authored.

### 3. Details Must Support Composition

The added details are not random ornaments.

They are used to:

- Balance empty space
- Guide the eye
- Frame the product
- Break centered symmetry
- Add editorial personality
- Make the page feel intentionally composed

Reusable principle:

Add details only where they solve a visual problem or strengthen the concept.

### 4. Space Can Make A Design Feel Premium

One composition felt premium because the text was not too big and had space around it.

Reusable principle:

Premium does not always mean louder. Sometimes the expensive move is restraint.

## Motion

### 1. Motion Comes Last

The creator adds animation only after typography, imagery, and composition already work.

This is important. Motion does not rescue weak static design.

Reusable principle:

Make the still design strong first. Then animate to enhance it.

### 2. Animation Makes The Final Design Feel Alive

The video shows a smooth intro animation applied to the finished hero.

It works because the elements are already well composed. The animation reveals and emphasizes the design instead of compensating for it.

Reusable principle:

Good motion is the final layer of polish, not the foundation.

### 3. Template-Based Animation Can Still Feel Premium

The creator uses Webflow/component tooling and still gets a premium result.

Reusable principle:

The tool does not make the work cheap. Generic decisions make the work cheap.

## Template Rules

### Good Use Of Templates

Use a template as:

- A speed scaffold
- A structural starting point
- A responsive layout base
- A way to avoid reinventing common patterns

Then customize:

- Type
- Copy
- Image direction
- Cropping
- Composition
- Details
- Motion
- Brand-specific symbolism

### Bad Use Of Templates

Do not use a template as:

- The final visual identity
- A substitute for art direction
- A reason to skip typography decisions
- A reason to use generic stock imagery
- A reason to avoid concept work

## Practical AI Agent Workflow

When starting from a template:

1. Identify the base layout pattern.
2. Strip away the assumption that the template is the final design.
3. Choose a typography direction before choosing imagery.
4. Write several headline/copy options and test them visually.
5. Build a non-web inspiration board.
6. Define the emotional world of the product or business.
7. Gather or generate imagery that supports that world.
8. Iterate images; reject weak outputs.
9. Combine type and image.
10. Adjust crop, focal point, contrast, and negative space.
11. Add only details that improve balance, meaning, or uniqueness.
12. Add motion after the static design works.
13. Check whether the template is still visible. If yes, the design decisions are not specific enough yet.

- Lighting

## What To Avoid

- Assuming a template makes the design amateur.
- Assuming a template makes the design good.
- Starting with AI imagery before having a visual direction.
- Accepting first AI outputs.
- Adding details just because the page feels empty.
- Adding motion before the static design works.
- Using premium fonts without checking readability and fit.
- Copying fashion/editorial style when the brand needs clarity and trust.
- Letting the product disappear behind the visual concept.

## Important Caveats For The Brain

This video uses AI-generated fashion/product imagery as a creative method. For real businesses, especially local cafes, restaurants, medical providers, and service businesses, real assets often build more trust than invented imagery.

Use AI imagery when:

- Concepting directions
- Building speculative visuals
- Creating abstract brand atmospheres
- Showing a product in a symbolic world

Prefer real imagery when:

- The audience needs trust
- The business has a physical space
- The team, food, product, or service is the proof
- The website will be published as an official brand site
