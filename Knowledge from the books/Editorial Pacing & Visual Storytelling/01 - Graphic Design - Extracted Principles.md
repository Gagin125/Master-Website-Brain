# Graphic Design - Extracted Principles

Source: Ellen Lupton, *Graphic Design*

Extraction quality: usable with some empty pages. The PDF is highly visual, but the extracted text clearly surfaced the main concepts around rhythm, balance, scale, color, hierarchy, grids, visual thinking, and pacing.

## Core Idea

Graphic design is visual thinking. It is not only styling elements; it is arranging form, image, text, rhythm, sequence, and contrast so the viewer knows how to move through meaning.

For websites, this means a page should feel composed as a journey, not assembled as isolated blocks.

## Key Principles

### 1. Visual language is built from basic elements

Design uses point, line, plane, shape, texture, color, scale, rhythm, balance, hierarchy, and grid.

Website translation:

- every section should have a dominant visual idea
- whitespace is an active design element
- typography and images should work as one composition
- repeated visual language creates identity

### 2. Rhythm controls movement through a page

Rhythm comes from repetition, variation, and interruption.

For websites:

- repeated section headers create continuity
- varied image sizes create motion
- alternating light/dark sections create cadence
- quiet sections let dramatic sections matter
- too much repetition becomes boring
- too much variation becomes chaotic

### 3. Balance does not require symmetry

The book discusses asymmetry as a route to compositional balance.

Website translation:

- a large image can balance a smaller text block
- a dark section can balance light sections
- a wide gallery image can balance smaller image clusters
- asymmetric layouts can feel premium when the underlying weight is controlled

### 4. Scale creates drama and hierarchy

Scale is not just size. It controls importance, intimacy, and visual energy.

Use scale to:

- make the hero memorable
- make section transitions feel intentional
- create editorial emphasis
- make proof or atmosphere feel substantial

Avoid:

- oversized type inside compact cards
- huge headings that wrap badly
- making every section equally large

### 5. Multipage and sequential formats require pacing

The extracted text explicitly mentions multipage formats and pacing. A sequence must distribute color, scale, image, and verbal information over time.

Website translation:

- section order matters
- not every section should have the same density
- proof should appear before big asks when trust is low
- emotional images should be placed where they support the story
- a gallery should feel curated, not dumped

### 6. Visual curation matters

Designers collect, edit, arrange, and curate visual/verbal material.

For websites:

- not every available photo belongs on the homepage
- real photos still need selection and cropping
- image order should tell a story
- captions or labels should be minimal unless they help
- repeated similar images weaken impact

### 7. Templates provide clarity but can flatten voice

Templates and grids clarify content, but installing ideas into templates can become generic unless curation and visual voice remain strong.

This maps directly to AI website work:

- use structure to avoid chaos
- avoid making every site feel template-produced
- create visual decisions from brand truth and assets

### 8. Color, image, and typography create atmosphere together

Color is not isolated from image and type. A palette can feel different depending on surrounding media, scale, and contrast.

For websites:

- choose colors after inspecting real assets
- gallery sections may need different backgrounds from review/menu sections
- dark sections can make images feel more cinematic
- cream sections can make hospitality feel warm
- contrast between sections creates scroll rhythm

## Website Translation

### Page pacing

A strong page often follows a rhythm like:

1. strong first impression
2. clear explanation
3. sensory or emotional proof
4. practical information
5. social proof
6. atmosphere/gallery
7. action/contact

This order can change by business, but the page should have a rhythm.

### Galleries

Galleries should be curated like an editorial spread:

- vary crops and sizes
- avoid generic uniform grids when atmosphere matters
- place best image first
- group by mood or subject
- avoid duplicate-feeling images
- give photos enough space to breathe

### Review sections

Review sections should often be calmer than galleries. Their job is trust and proof, not visual drama.

### Menu sections

Menu sections should use strong structure. They should not become too visually loud because users need to compare items and prices.

### Section contrast

Adjacent sections should not accidentally blend into one flat block. Use contrast through:

- background color shift
- spacing change
- scale change
- image density change
- layout change

Do this intentionally, not every section.

## Common AI Website Mistakes

- stacking sections with the same background and spacing
- making every section a centered title plus cards
- using galleries as photo dumps
- using too many dramatic sections in a row
- failing to create quiet pauses
- changing color without considering real images
- treating each section independently instead of as page sequence

## Candidate Rules For The Brain

- A page needs cadence, not just sections.
- Rhythm is repetition plus variation.
- Balance can be asymmetric.
- Use scale to create hierarchy and emotional force.
- Curate images; do not dump all available assets.
- Adjacent sections need enough contrast to feel intentional.
- Structure gives clarity; curation gives voice.

## Rules Not To Overgeneralize

- Do not make every site look like a magazine.
- Do not add dramatic pacing where users need fast utility.
- Do not force dark sections if the brand needs softness.
- Do not make every section contrast strongly; too much contrast becomes noisy.
