# The Photographer's Eye - Extracted Principles

Source: Michael Freeman, *The Photographer's Eye*  
Extraction quality: Usable. The PDF text extracted well. The book is highly visual, so these notes translate composition principles into website art-direction rules.

## Core Idea

A photograph is built inside a frame. The frame, subject, edges, balance, geometry, and moment determine how the viewer's eye moves.

For websites, photos must be composed and cropped as part of the layout, not dropped in after the design is finished.

## Practical Principles

### 1. The Image Frame Has Force

The edges of the frame influence what feels important, cut off, balanced, or tense.

Website use:
- Check image edges after responsive cropping.
- Do not accidentally cut off faces, products, signs, plates, or important environmental cues.
- A deliberate edge crop can feel editorial; an accidental crop feels amateur.

### 2. Composition Is Selection

Photography selects from reality. The designer must decide what reality matters for the user.

Website use:
- For a restaurant, show food, interior, service, and atmosphere, not random decor.
- For a local business demo, use photos that help the owner recognize their brand.
- For a sales page, choose images that reduce doubt and make the offer concrete.

### 3. Geometry Creates Order

Lines, rectangles, diagonals, circles, and repeated shapes create visual rhythm.

Website use:
- Match image geometry to the section structure.
- Use horizontal images for wide hero or gallery bands.
- Use vertical images for editorial split layouts.
- Avoid forcing a vertical subject into a horizontal container unless the crop still tells the story.

### 4. Balance Is Not Always Symmetry

An image can feel balanced through visual weight, contrast, subject placement, or empty space.

Website use:
- Asymmetry can make a page feel premium if hierarchy remains clear.
- Use large/small image pairings to create editorial pacing.
- Avoid layouts where all images have identical weight unless the goal is calm catalog browsing.

### 5. The Subject Needs A Relationship To The Frame

A subject placed dead center can feel stable, formal, or static. A subject placed off-center can create movement, tension, or room for copy.

Website use:
- Use center placement for product inspection.
- Use off-center placement for hero text overlays.
- Use gaze direction or object direction to lead toward CTA or headline.

### 6. Cropping Can Change Meaning

The same photo can feel intimate, documentary, luxurious, crowded, or vague depending on crop.

Website use:
- Create separate desktop and mobile crop rules when needed.
- If a product is too low, high, or hidden, adjust object-position.
- Crop by meaning, not by container convenience.

### 7. Moment Matters

A photograph can capture tension, action, pause, anticipation, or completion.

Website use:
- People photos should show a real moment, not only posed presence.
- Service businesses benefit from images showing the work happening.
- Hospitality benefits from images showing the experience in progress.

### 8. Composition Should Support The Page Goal

The same image can be good art but bad web communication if it distracts from the page's job.

Website use:
- Hero image: quickly establish what the business is and why it matters.
- Menu/product section: show the offer clearly.
- Trust section: show proof, people, process, or place.
- Gallery: build brand world and depth.

## Website Checklist

- What is the first thing the eye sees?
- Does the frame cut anything important?
- Does the image shape fit the layout shape?
- Does the composition help the user understand the section?
- Is the crop intentional on phone, tablet, laptop, and desktop?
- Is there enough visual quiet for text and buttons?
- Is the image overused elsewhere on the page?

## What To Avoid

- Same-size photo grids when the brand needs editorial rhythm.
- Random object-position fixes without checking the meaning of the crop.
- Treating gallery images as fillers.
- Using images that fight the headline.
- Cropping for aesthetic balance while hiding the actual product.

## Rule To Reuse Later

Design the crop, not just the component. A photo becomes part of the interface only after its frame, focal point, and responsive behavior are chosen deliberately.
