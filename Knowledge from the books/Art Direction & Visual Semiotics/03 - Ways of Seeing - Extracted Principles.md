# Ways of Seeing - Extracted Principles

Source: John Berger, *Ways of Seeing*  
Extraction quality: Usable. The text extracted clearly enough for conceptual principles. The book is cultural and critical rather than tactical, so these notes focus on image meaning, context, and ethical art direction.

## Core Idea

Images are not neutral. Every image embodies a way of seeing, and the meaning of an image changes with context, reproduction, surrounding text, and the viewer's expectations.

For websites, imagery is not just visual decoration. It shapes trust, desire, status, identity, and interpretation before the user reads the copy.

## Practical Principles

### 1. Seeing Comes Before Words

Users form a visual impression before they fully read.

Website use:
- The first screen must visually communicate category, quality, and trust.
- The hero image or video should confirm the headline immediately.
- If the visual says one thing and the copy says another, the visual usually wins.

### 2. Images Carry A Point Of View

A photo reflects a selection: who is shown, what is hidden, what is centered, what is made desirable.

Website use:
- Choose images that reflect the real brand's point of view.
- Do not use visuals that make the business feel more luxurious, diverse, local, ethical, or active than it really is.
- Avoid over-polished stock imagery when real imperfection would build more trust.

### 3. Context Changes Meaning

The same image means different things depending on where it appears, what text surrounds it, and what the user expects.

Website use:
- A coffee cup in a hero section can say "calm pause"; in a menu section it can say "product"; in a review section it can say "proof."
- A dark luxury photo can feel premium on a fine dining site, but cold or unfriendly on a neighborhood cafe site.
- Add captions or surrounding copy when the image needs interpretation.

### 4. Reproduction Weakens And Multiplies Meaning

Once an image is reused, cropped, copied, or placed in a new context, it can lose its original meaning and gain a new one.

Website use:
- Reused Google/stock/customer photos should be handled carefully.
- A demo can use public-facing materials for mockup thinking, but published work should use approved assets.
- Repeating the same image across many sections reduces its impact.

### 5. Publicity Imagery Often Sells Transformation

Advertising images often suggest that the viewer can become more desirable, successful, refined, relaxed, or included by buying.

Website use:
- Good website imagery can show a believable after-state.
- The promise should be grounded in the real product or service.
- Do not manipulate through fake status signals, fake scarcity, or fake social proof.

### 6. The Viewer Is Part Of The Meaning

Users bring their own expectations, cultural knowledge, anxieties, and desires.

Website use:
- A luxury audience may read restraint as confidence.
- A local cafe audience may read too much luxury as distance.
- A skeptical B2B audience may need proof higher on the page.
- A family audience may need warmth and practicality, not only style.

### 7. Visual Ethics Matter

Images can objectify, exaggerate, exclude, or mislead. Those choices may create short-term attention but damage trust.

Website use:
- Use real reviews and real people responsibly.
- Avoid images that imply endorsements, ownership, or official representation without approval.
- Avoid making a demo site look officially published by the business unless it is clearly part of an approved process.

## Website Checklist

- What does this image make the business appear to be?
- Is that appearance true?
- What does the image promise emotionally?
- Does the surrounding copy change the image's meaning?
- Could the image be misleading if separated from context?
- Is this image overused, generic, or disconnected from the brand?
- Would a real customer recognize the place, product, or experience?

## What To Avoid

- Treating images as neutral.
- Using photos only because they look expensive.
- Letting AI/stock imagery erase the brand's human reality.
- Creating visual promises the business cannot fulfill.
- Ignoring how images will be read by the target audience.

## Rule To Reuse Later

Every image says, "This is what this brand values." Choose images only after deciding what value, proof, or feeling the section must communicate.
