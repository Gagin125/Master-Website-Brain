# Designing for Emotion - Extracted Principles

Source: Aaron Walter, *Designing for Emotion*

Extraction quality: usable. The extracted text clearly surfaced the central emotional-design framework.

## Core Idea

Usability is not the ceiling of design. A website must first be functional, reliable, and usable. Once those needs are met, personality, delight, and emotional connection can make the experience memorable.

For websites, this means emotion is not decoration. Emotion is a strategic layer added after the basics work.

## Key Principles

### 1. Emotional design sits on top of functional design

The book adapts the idea of human needs to interface design:

1. functional
2. reliable
3. usable
4. pleasurable / delightful / memorable

Website translation:

- a beautiful video hero is useless if it loads too slowly
- clever animation is harmful if navigation breaks
- personality is weak if the user cannot find the menu or directions
- delight should never replace clarity

### 2. Personality creates human connection

Websites can feel like they were made by humans, not machines.

Personality can show through:

- copy tone
- photography
- microinteractions
- small details
- layout rhythm
- illustrations or icons
- moments of warmth

But personality must fit the brand. A bank, fine-dining restaurant, family cafe, SaaS tool, and barber shop should not all sound or move the same.

### 3. Emotion strengthens memory

Emotionally charged experiences are more memorable.

Website translation:

- one strong visual moment can make a site stick
- real human details increase memory
- small moments of delight can increase trust
- atmosphere matters when the product is experiential

### 4. Delight is long-term, not just surprise

A gimmick can surprise once. Real delight continues to feel good after repeated use.

Good delight:

- smooth interaction
- helpful feedback
- elegant language switch
- real photos that feel warm
- quick loading
- small animation that confirms action

Bad delight:

- over-the-top animation
- hidden navigation
- slow transitions
- motion that competes with the offer

### 5. Emotional design should understand the user's state

The emotional design should match how the user feels when arriving.

Examples:

- hungry user: wants clarity, menu, hours, booking
- nervous buyer: wants proof and reassurance
- luxury diner: wants confidence and atmosphere
- busy parent: wants practicality and trust
- enterprise buyer: wants competence and risk reduction

### 6. Human touch counters generic machine feel

The extracted text frames emotional design as preserving human quality in a mechanized web.

For the AI brain, this is central:

- real assets matter
- specific copy matters
- brand-fit interaction matters
- one-size-fits-all design becomes forgettable

## Website Translation

### Emotional layer checklist

Before adding delight, confirm:

- page loads fast enough
- nav is clear
- CTAs work
- mobile works
- text is readable
- key information is findable

Then add:

- subtle motion
- brand-specific copy
- sensory imagery
- warmer transitions
- memorable section contrast
- personality in microcopy

### Hospitality websites

Emotional design is especially useful because restaurants and cafes sell experience, not just information.

Use:

- real food/drink photos
- atmosphere images
- warm pacing
- reviews
- clear practical info
- gentle motion

### SaaS or utility websites

Use emotional design more carefully:

- reduce anxiety
- celebrate progress
- make empty states helpful
- keep controls predictable

## Common AI Website Mistakes

- adding delight before usability
- using generic "fun" copy for serious brands
- adding animation because it looks premium
- confusing emotional design with decoration
- making every website expressive in the same way
- ignoring the user's emotional state

## Candidate Rules For The Brain

- Function, reliability, and usability come before delight.
- Emotion makes websites memorable when it fits the user's state.
- Personality must be brand-specific.
- Delight should be useful over repeated visits, not just surprising once.
- Human touch is a defense against generic AI design.
- Motion should support emotion and clarity.

## Rules Not To Overgeneralize

- Do not force playful personality onto every brand.
- Do not make emotional design louder than functional content.
- Do not assume delight always means animation.
- Do not use surprise when reassurance is needed.
