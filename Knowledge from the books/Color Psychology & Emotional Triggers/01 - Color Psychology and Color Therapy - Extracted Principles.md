# Color Psychology and Color Therapy - Extracted Principles

Source: Faber Birren, *Color Psychology and Color Therapy: A Factual Study of the Influence of Color on Human Life*

Extraction quality: usable. Important caution: this book includes historical, psychological, environmental, and therapeutic claims. For the website brain, therapeutic/medical claims should not be treated as reliable design rules. The useful material is mostly about associations, environment, perception, mood, safety, preference, and functional color use.

## Core Idea

Color affects perception, mood, attention, and association, but not in a simple universal way. Color works through light, contrast, environment, culture, memory, symbolism, and practical function.

For websites, color should be used as a communication system, not as magic emotional control.

## Key Principles

### 1. Color has psychological force, but context changes it

The book treats color as influential in human life, but also shows many examples where meaning depends on environment and association.

Website translation:

- red can feel energetic, appetizing, urgent, dangerous, ceremonial, or cheap depending on context
- green can feel natural, calm, medical, financial, religious, or dated
- blue can feel trustworthy, cold, sacred, corporate, or calm
- yellow can feel cheerful, sacred, warning-like, childish, or harsh

Never assign one universal meaning to a hue.

### 2. Associations can overpower color theory

One example in the extracted text describes green billiard tables being associated with pool halls and gambling, causing resistance in a home context. Changing the color changed perception.

Principle:

User association matters more than abstract theory.

For websites:

- a color may be technically good but culturally wrong
- a palette may be beautiful but category-inappropriate
- local audience expectations matter
- competitor colors can change what a color communicates

### 3. Environmental color affects perceived comfort

The book discusses color in workplaces, hospitals, industrial settings, and interiors.

Website translation:

Color affects the perceived atmosphere of a digital place:

- warm neutrals can make hospitality feel calmer
- muted greens can feel restorative
- deep dark palettes can feel cinematic or exclusive
- harsh high-saturation palettes can feel noisy
- too much white can feel sterile

But the website should still match the real place or product.

### 4. Functional color is different from emotional color

Color can serve practical functions:

- safety coding
- attention
- categorization
- orientation
- status
- action

For websites:

- CTA color should be reserved for action
- active tabs should be clearly distinct
- warning/error/success states should be consistent
- color should not be the only signal

### 5. Warm and cool colors shift energy

Warm colors often feel closer, more active, and more stimulating. Cool colors often feel more distant, calmer, or more restrained.

Use carefully:

- warm hospitality palettes can support appetite and comfort
- cool palettes can support calm, technology, healthcare, or trust
- too much warmth can feel heavy or dated
- too much coolness can feel cold or impersonal

### 6. Lightness and saturation matter as much as hue

The emotional effect of a color depends heavily on:

- brightness
- saturation
- contrast
- surrounding colors
- image content
- texture
- amount used

The same green can feel premium, cheap, organic, clinical, or institutional depending on value and saturation.

### 7. Color therapy claims should not become website rules

The book contains claims around therapy and physical/mental effects. These are not appropriate as direct web-design rules.

For the brain:

- do not claim color cures, heals, or guarantees emotion
- do not treat color psychology as hard conversion science
- do not use medical-like claims unless verified by modern credible evidence

## Website Translation

### Hospitality

Useful color goals:

- appetite
- warmth
- calm
- trust
- atmosphere
- authenticity

Good inputs:

- real interior photos
- food/drink colors
- lighting
- materials
- customer expectations

### Premium brands

Premium color usually comes from discipline:

- fewer colors
- controlled contrast
- muted saturation
- high-quality neutrals
- one or two accents

It does not automatically come from black, gold, or purple.

### CTAs

CTA color should:

- contrast enough to be found
- appear consistently
- not be used on non-clickable decoration
- change subtly on hover/press

## Common AI Website Mistakes

- saying "red increases appetite" as if it always applies
- using green for every natural/healthy brand
- using blue for every trust brand
- using black/gold for every premium site
- choosing colors without checking photos
- using too many accent colors
- making low-contrast text because the palette looks soft
- relying on color alone for state

## Candidate Rules For The Brain

- Color psychology is contextual, not universal.
- Associations can overpower theory.
- Functional color must be consistent.
- Hue, saturation, brightness, and amount all matter.
- Real assets should guide palette decisions.
- CTA color is a behavior signal and should be protected.
- Treat color therapy claims as non-actionable for websites.

## Rules Not To Overgeneralize

- Do not use color to manipulate emotion as if users are machines.
- Do not make medical or guaranteed psychological claims from color.
- Do not assume one color meaning across cultures and categories.
- Do not sacrifice readability for mood.
