## Core Thesis

People are not perfectly rational decision-makers. Their choices are shaped by context, comparison, emotion, expectations, ownership, social norms, and the way options are presented.

For websites:

> The page does not only present information. It creates the comparison environment in which the visitor decides.

---

## Website-Relevant Ideas

### 1. Relativity And Comparison

People evaluate options relative to nearby alternatives, not in isolation.

Website translation:

- Pricing tables shape perceived value.
- Menu structure shapes what feels premium or normal.
- Product/service comparisons can make choices easier.
- Showing a "best for most people" option can reduce confusion.

Good use:

- Clear service tiers with meaningful differences.
- Menu categories that help scanning.
- Highlighting the most relevant choice, not the most profitable one by trick.

Bad use:

- Confusing comparison tables.
- Manipulative decoy options.
- Hiding important limitations until after the decision.

---

### 2. Anchoring

The first number, option, or framing point can influence later judgments.

Website translation:

- The first price a visitor sees can shape what feels expensive or reasonable.
- Premium visual quality can anchor perceived business quality.
- A high-quality hero can set the trust baseline.

Good use:

- Show credible quality signals early.
- Use pricing context honestly.
- Present value before price when the offer needs understanding.

Bad use:

- Artificially inflated "was" prices.
- Fake premium positioning unsupported by product/service quality.

---

### 3. The Power And Risk Of "Free"

"Free" changes behavior strongly. It can reduce friction, but it can also attract low-intent users or cheapen a premium brand.

Website translation:

- Free guides, audits, samples, trials, or consultations can work when they are strategically real.
- For premium brands, free should feel generous, not desperate.

Good use:

- Free menu viewing.
- Free checklist with real value.
- Free consultation only when it helps qualify and guide.

Bad use:

- "Free" everywhere.
- Low-value lead magnets.
- Free offers that create poor-quality leads.

---

### 4. Social Norms Vs Market Norms

People behave differently when an interaction feels social/community-based versus purely transactional.

Website translation:

- Local cafes, restaurants, nonprofits, communities, schools, and personal brands can benefit from warmth and belonging.
- Luxury services and premium offers must balance human warmth with professionalism.

Good use:

- A cafe can feel neighborhood-based and human.
- A fine dining restaurant can feel hospitable without becoming casual.
- A service business can explain its care and process without sounding robotic.

Bad use:

- Overly transactional copy for a community/local brand.
- Overly intimate copy for a high-trust professional service.

---

### 5. Ownership And The Endowment Effect

People value things more once they feel ownership or personal connection.

Website translation:

- Let visitors imagine themselves in the experience.
- Use real photos that make the environment/product feel tangible.
- Show the outcome after purchase/visit/service.

Good use:

- Restaurant photos that show the room, table, dish, mood.
- Service copy that helps the visitor picture the improved state.
- Configurators/previews when useful.

Bad use:

- Fake personalization.
- Overpromising transformation.

---

### 6. Expectations Shape Experience

Presentation affects perceived quality. People often experience products through the lens of expectation.

Website translation:

- A premium website raises expectations.
- Sloppy design lowers trust before the user reads.
- Photography, typography, spacing, and copy set the expected quality level.

Important warning:

- Do not create expectations the business cannot meet.
- A site should elevate perception, but not lie.

---

### 7. Emotion And Decision Timing

People make worse decisions under strong emotion, pressure, or arousal.

Website translation:

- Avoid exploiting anxiety.
- Avoid aggressive countdowns, shame copy, or fear-heavy conversion pressure.
- Calm, clear design often performs better for trust-heavy decisions.

Good use:

- Help people decide confidently.
- Make the next step easy.
- Use reassurance where risk is high.

Bad use:

- Panic-based urgency.
- Manipulative fear language.

## Practical Website Rules Extracted

- Design the comparison set intentionally.
- Present prices with context, not confusion.
- Use "free" carefully; it changes perceived value.
- Match tone to the relationship type: social, premium, professional, local, or transactional.
- Help visitors imagine ownership/outcome.
- Use premium presentation to create positive expectations, but do not overpromise.
- Avoid emotional manipulation.

---

## Pricing And Offer Lessons

When designing pricing:

- Make differences between tiers meaningful.
- Avoid too many options.
- Use anchors honestly.
- Make the recommended option genuinely useful for the target customer.
- Make price and value easy to compare.
- Do not hide critical costs.

When designing menus:

- Category structure guides attention.
- Premium items may need better descriptions.
- Line-based price layouts help price scan without making the page feel cheap.
- Too many items without hierarchy can create choice fatigue.

- A website creates the comparison context, not just the content.
- Pricing and options must be structured to reduce confusion.
- Premium presentation shapes expectations before copy is read.
- "Free" is powerful but can damage positioning when used carelessly.
- Let users imagine the experience through real assets and outcome-focused copy.
