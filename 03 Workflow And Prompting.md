# Workflow And Prompting

## Most Important Workflow Lesson

The strongest websites came from conversation and planning, not one huge prompt.

The best workflow discovered:

1. Talk first.
2. Compare references.
3. Identify what works and what does not.
4. Decide the section purpose.
5. Make a checklist.
6. User says "Begin."
7. AI edits only the agreed files and scope.
8. Verify.
9. Iterate visually.

The weak workflow:

- User gives a large vague request.
- AI makes a whole page at once.
- The result becomes generic.
- Fixing it takes longer than planning would have taken.

Working principle:

Planning for one or two hours can save many hours of repair.

## Knowledge Selection Workflow

Before designing, do not dump every book and video principle into the prompt.

Use this selection order:

1. Define the business type and main user action.
2. Identify the user's mental state: desire, skepticism, urgency, confidence, fear, or curiosity.
3. Choose only the book principles that match that mental state.
4. Choose only the video/design patterns that match the business assets and user task.
5. Reject any tactic that harms clarity, trust, accessibility, speed, or mobile usability.
6. Turn the selected principles into a short section-by-section checklist.
7. Only then begin implementation.

Best prompt addition:

```text
Use the Obsidian brain as judgment, not as a pile of instructions.
From the books, apply only the principles that fit this user's psychology.
From the videos, apply only the visual patterns that fit this brand and section goal.
If a principle conflicts with clarity, trust, performance, accessibility, or the agreed scope, do not use it.
```

## Prompting Formula That Worked

Best structure for future requests:

```text
We are just talking. Do not code.

Section: [section name]
Current problem: [what feels wrong]
Goal: [what should feel better]
What must stay unchanged: [design/copy/layout/colors]
Reference: [image/site/example]
My current thought: [idea]

Tell me if this is a good idea or if you would do it differently.
```

Then after planning:

```text
Give me a checklist of the exact things that need to change.
```

Then:

```text
Begin.
Only change the checklist items.
Do not redesign unrelated sections.
```

For implementation:

```text
Apply these changes:
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...

Files/sections involved:
- ...

Preserve:
- ...
```

## Synthesis Planning Prompt

Use this when a new design direction is unclear:

```text
We are just talking. Do not code.

Project:
Business type:
Main user action:
Real assets available:
Audience/customer psychology:
Brand feeling:
What must stay practical:
What can be expressive:

Compare the relevant book principles and video patterns.
Tell me what applies, what conflicts, what to reject, and what section plan you recommend.
```

## Project Start Discovery Framework

For every new local business website, start with these questions internally:

### Business Reality

- What business type is it?
- What is the main offer?
- What is the main conversion action?
- What does the customer need to do quickly?
- Is the purchase urgent, emotional, routine, premium, or social?

### Brand Reality

- What do the photos say about the brand?
- Is it warm, luxury, playful, utilitarian, family-oriented, modern, traditional?
- What colors already exist in signage, interior, product, uniforms, packaging?
- Is the business cheap, mid-range, premium, or elite?

### Trust Reality

- What proof exists?
- Reviews?
- Real customer photos?
- Before/after photos?
- Team photos?
- Awards?
- Location credibility?

### Website Role

- Should this be a booking engine?
- A call-first local service site?
- A menu/location site?
- A portfolio/lookbook?
- A trust/reputation repair site?
- A simple one-page conversion site?
