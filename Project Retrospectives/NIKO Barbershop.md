# NIKO Barbershop Retrospective

## What Worked

- Real black-and-white brand photos gave the strongest direction.
- The new hero image improved brand fit immediately.
- Serif typography made the barbershop feel more premium and less generic.
- Removing the trust strip improved the flow from hero into team.
- Removing the reviews section improved the visual standard because the section looked too vibe-coded.
- Team cards became stronger after removing price lists, heavy buttons, and extra description text.
- Single-column services made pricing easier to read and more premium.
- The final booking section made sense because booking is the primary action.

## What Failed

- The AI agreed too quickly and made the user carry too much creative direction.
- Candidate sections were treated too much like required sections.
- Trust strip felt generic and interrupted the site.
- Reviews looked pasted in instead of native to the brand.
- The gallery carousel hover behavior was broken because hover reset/jumped instead of slowing naturally.
- FAQ animation looked cheap when answers appeared/disappeared instead of opening smoothly.
- Over-polishing before locking brand diagnosis caused wasted cycles.

## What The User Liked

- Dark-to-photo hero blend.
- Brand-specific design based on real photos.
- Premium serif typography.
- Clean black/cream contrast.
- Gallery/results that clearly show haircuts.
- Booking as the final destination.
- Smooth, intentional motion.
- Challenging ideas instead of yes-man agreement.

## What The User Rejected

- Generic trust strip.
- Generic testimonials section.
- Playful language toggle blob.
- Too much cream without enough dark rhythm.
- Gold prices when gold was not supported by the brand.
- Harsh or muddy gradient transitions between sections.
- Too many blocky cards and borders.
- Fast pop-in animations.

## Rules To Remember

- Diagnose brand before choosing sections.
- Candidate sections are not mandatory sections.
- Real photos should decide the visual system.
- If a section feels generic, remove it or redesign it deeply.
- Do not build every demo like a paid production site.
- For barbershops with barber choice, team and services matter more than generic trust rows.
- If the user is unsure, give a verdict instead of asking vague questions.
- AI must challenge weak design decisions before coding.
